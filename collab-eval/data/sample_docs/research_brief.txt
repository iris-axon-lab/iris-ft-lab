Evaluating Agentic Systems: Open Problems

Evaluating agentic AI systems presents fundamentally different challenges than evaluating
static language models. Agentic systems take sequences of actions, interact with external
tools, and produce outcomes that unfold over time. This means that performance cannot be
captured by scoring a single output against a gold label.

One key challenge is that reward signals for agentic tasks are often sparse and delayed.
An agent may complete 90% of a multi-step task correctly but fail at the final step, and
a naive scorer that measures only final-state success will fail to distinguish a near-miss
from a complete failure. Intermediate reward shaping is therefore essential for training
stable policies.

A second challenge is that agents operating in document-manipulation tasks tend to overfit
to surface-level formatting cues rather than semantic correctness. Empirical studies have
shown that models fine-tuned on document tasks consistently score higher on format-compliance
metrics than on factual faithfulness metrics, even when format compliance is not the
intended objective.

Addressing these challenges requires evaluation frameworks that decompose reward across
multiple dimensions and apply deterministic checks wherever the ground truth is recoverable,
reserving model-based judgment for dimensions where no algorithmic check is feasible.
